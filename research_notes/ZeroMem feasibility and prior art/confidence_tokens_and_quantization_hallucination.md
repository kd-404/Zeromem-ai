# Confidence/Abstention Special Tokens (Part A) and Quantization vs. Hallucination (Part B)

## PART A: What does Self-RAG do with its reflection tokens, and how similar is this to a "KNOW/UNSURE/REFUSE/DONE" scheme?

### Takeaway
Self-RAG (Asai et al., 2023, arXiv:2310.11511) is the closest well-established precedent for a "special control tokens trained into the weights to govern retrieval/generation flow and self-grade output" scheme, but its tokens are about *retrieval control and post-hoc self-critique of support/relevance/utility*, not about the model declaring its own epistemic confidence state ("I know" vs. "I'm unsure") before answering. A "KNOW/UNSURE/REFUSE/DONE" scheme would be a novel combination that borrows Self-RAG's mechanism (special tokens co-trained with LM next-token prediction, emitted inline in the generation stream) but repurposes it toward explicit confidence/abstention signaling, which is closer in spirit to R-Tuning and "selective prediction" work than to Self-RAG itself.

### Cited Findings
- Self-RAG trains one LM to generate both the response text and interspersed "reflection tokens" from four categories: `[Retrieve]` (should the model retrieve more passages, yes/no/continue), `[IsRel]` (is a retrieved passage relevant to the query), `[IsSup]` (is a generated statement fully/partially/not supported by the cited passage), and `[IsUse]` (is the overall response useful, on a 1–5 scale) — [arXiv:2310.11511](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11511)
- These tokens are predicted as ordinary vocabulary tokens by the same LM, trained end-to-end via a critic-model-distillation procedure (a separate "critic" LM, e.g. GPT-4-prompted, first labels training data with these tokens, then a generator LM is fine-tuned to predict text + reflection tokens jointly) — [arXiv:2310.11511](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11511), [GitHub AkariAsai/self-rag](https://github.com/AkariAsai/self-rag/blob/main/README.md)
- The tokens are used at inference time for two purposes: (1) adaptively deciding whether/when to retrieve (`[Retrieve]`) instead of retrieving a fixed number of passages every time, and (2) segment-level self-critique/reranking — the model generates multiple candidate continuations per retrieved passage and uses `[IsRel]`/`[IsSup]`/`[IsUse]` scores to select or weight the best one — [arXiv:2310.11511](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11511)
- Self-RAG's design goal is explicitly framed as making retrieval and generation "controllable" and improving factuality/citation quality versus vanilla RAG and vanilla instruction-tuned LMs — [Self-RAG summary via search](https://learnprompting.org/docs/retrieval_augmented_generation/self-rag)

### Inferences
- Self-RAG's tokens are *process-control and self-critique* signals (retrieve-or-not; is this passage/claim supported/useful), evaluated per-segment and often used for re-ranking multiple candidate generations — they are not a single top-level "confidence state" token emitted once to gate whether the model answers or abstains. A KNOW/UNSURE/REFUSE/DONE scheme is structurally analogous (special tokens co-trained with the base LM, interleaved in the output stream, used to control generation/retrieval flow) but semantically different in what the tokens represent (epistemic confidence/abstention decision vs. retrieval-need and support/relevance grading).
- The "DONE" concept in a KNOW/UNSURE/REFUSE/DONE scheme (i.e., "I have enough evidence, stop retrieving") is functionally closest to Self-RAG's `[Retrieve]=no` decision, and to explicit "stop retrieving" tokens used in RL-trained agentic search models like Search-R1 (see below), rather than to anything in RAGTruth or R-Tuning.

### Gaps
- No paper found that proposes exactly a "KNOW/UNSURE/REFUSE/DONE" 4-way token vocabulary as such; this appears to be a novel proposed scheme rather than one already published, based on this search.

## What does RAGTruth do, and is there associated training work using special tokens?

### Takeaway
RAGTruth (Niu et al., 2024, arXiv:2401.00396, ACL 2024) is a **hallucination annotation dataset/benchmark**, not a special-token training method. It does not itself introduce or train any control/confidence tokens; its stated use is to fine-tune smaller detector models (as a post-hoc hallucination classifier) or to evaluate prompt-based detection methods (e.g., GPT-4-as-judge) — the detection is applied externally to already-generated RAG outputs, not embedded as tokens the generator emits during decoding.

### Cited Findings
- RAGTruth consists of ~18,000 naturally generated responses from six LLMs (GPT-3.5-turbo-0613, GPT-4-0613, Mistral-7B-Instruct, and three Llama-2-chat variants) across three RAG task types: question answering, data-to-text generation, and news summarization, with manual span-level hallucination annotations plus hallucination-intensity labels — [arXiv:2401.00396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00396), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.585/)
- The paper's stated purpose is to benchmark hallucination frequency across different LLMs and to assess existing hallucination-detection methodologies, enabling development of hallucination-detection systems (as opposed to inline model self-signaling) — [arXiv:2401.00396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00396)
- The authors show that fine-tuning a relatively small LLM directly on the RAGTruth dataset lets it reach hallucination-detection performance competitive with prompting large models like GPT-4 for the same detection task — [WebFetch summary of arXiv:2401.00396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00396)
- Follow-on work built on RAGTruth includes "LettuceDetect" (arXiv:2502.17125), a token-classification-style hallucination detector, and "ReDeEP" (arXiv:2410.11414), a mechanistic-interpretability approach to detecting hallucination in RAG — these are external detectors evaluated/trained on RAGTruth-style data, not generator-side control tokens — [arXiv:2502.17125](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17125v1), [arXiv:2410.11414](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.11414)

### Inferences
- RAGTruth and its derivatives represent the "external/discriminative detection" branch of hallucination mitigation (label spans of already-generated text as hallucinated), which is a fundamentally different architecture from the "generative/self-signaling" branch (Self-RAG, R-Tuning, a KNOW/UNSURE/REFUSE scheme) where the generator itself is trained to emit its own confidence signal inline. No evidence found that RAGTruth-trained models use special vocabulary tokens; detection is framed as span classification/labeling over normal text.

### Gaps
- Did not find a paper that explicitly combines RAGTruth-style hallucination span labels with special-token training of a generator (i.e., using RAGTruth annotations as supervision to teach a model to emit an inline "this span may be hallucinated" token). This looks like an open combination not yet published, based on this search.

## Is there published work training LLMs to emit explicit abstention via special control tokens (vs. natural-language refusal)? (R-Tuning, Toolformer analogy, "Know What You Don't Know")

### Takeaway
R-Tuning (Zhang et al., NAACL 2024 Outstanding Paper, arXiv:2311.09677) is the clearest well-established precedent for training abstention behavior via fine-tuning on refusal-aware data, but it trains the model to produce natural-language refusal ("I am not sure," "I don't know" style continuations) driven by an uncertainty label, not a dedicated single-token vocabulary item comparable to Self-RAG's bracketed tokens. Toolformer (Schick et al., 2023, arXiv:2302.04761) is the best structural analogy for how a "control token that changes generation flow" can be trained self-supervised, but it targets tool/API invocation, not confidence/abstention.

### Cited Findings
- R-Tuning's core idea: prior instruction-tuning forces the model to complete an answer regardless of whether the fact is in its parametric knowledge, which drives hallucination when a query falls outside what the model actually knows; R-Tuning first measures the gap between the pretrained model's actual knowledge and the instruction-tuning data's assumed knowledge, then constructs "refusal-aware" training data only for the certain/uncertain split, and fine-tunes the model to append an uncertainty expression when its own knowledge doesn't support an answer — [arXiv:2311.09677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09677), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.394/)
- R-Tuning improves precision/recall on both in-domain and out-of-domain uncertain questions, and the refusal ability was shown to generalize as a "meta-skill" transferable to other tasks/domains not seen during refusal-aware tuning — [arXiv:2311.09677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09677)
- R-Tuning received the NAACL 2024 Outstanding Paper Award — [GitHub shizhediao/R-Tuning](https://github.com/shizhediao/R-Tuning)
- Toolformer trains a model, self-supervised, to decide which APIs to call, when, with what arguments, and how to use results, by having the model generate candidate API calls, execute them, and keep only the calls (via a self-supervised filtering loss) that measurably improve the model's next-token prediction; it represents calls in-line in text using literal bracket/arrow tokens (e.g. `[`, `]`, `->` standing in for `<API>`, `</API>`, "→") rather than dedicated reserved vocabulary tokens with special embeddings — [arXiv:2302.04761](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04761), [emergentmind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/toolformer)
- Toolformer's mechanism (self-supervised bootstrapping: generate candidate token/call insertions, score by whether they help predict the continuation, filter, and fine-tune on the filtered/augmented corpus) is a generalizable recipe that in principle could be applied to train confidence-signal tokens (e.g., insert a candidate `[KNOW]`/`[UNSURE]` token, filter by whether it correlates with actual correctness, fine-tune) — but no paper found that has actually done this for confidence/abstention tokens specifically — [arXiv:2302.04761](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04761)
- Broader "selective prediction" / calibration literature (2023–2026) frames abstention as a risk-coverage trade-off and largely operates via post-hoc confidence scores, self-evaluation prompting ("Self-Evaluation Improves Selective Generation," arXiv:2312.09300), or fine-tuning objectives (SelectLLM, OpenReview) rather than dedicated single vocabulary tokens; e.g. one method reformulates the task so the model can pick a "None of the above" option to express uncertainty explicitly within its normal answer choices, again not via a reserved special token — [arXiv:2312.09300](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09300v1), [SelectLLM/OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=JJPAy8mvrQ)

### Inferences
- There is a real, well-established line of work (R-Tuning and the broader "say I don't know" / selective-prediction literature) establishing that training-time supervision can make models abstain rather than hallucinate, and this is treated as an improvement over relying on natural-language refusal learned incidentally through RLHF/safety tuning. However, the *specific implementation detail* of using a small set of dedicated, reserved special tokens (as opposed to natural-language refusal phrases or answer-option reformulation) for the KNOW/UNSURE/REFUSE part of the proposed scheme does not appear to have direct precedent in R-Tuning or the selective-prediction literature — the closest token-based precedent remains Self-RAG's reflection tokens and Toolformer's/Search-R1's control tokens (see below), which govern *process* (retrieve/critique/search) rather than a standalone confidence declaration.
- This is a gap worth flagging explicitly to the report writer: the "special reserved token trained into weights to declare confidence state" idea sits at the intersection of two established but previously separate approaches (Self-RAG/Toolformer/Search-R1's control-token mechanism, and R-Tuning/selective-prediction's abstention objective) — combining them appears to be a novel proposal, not something already published under this or a similar name, based on this search.

### Gaps
- No paper found using the literal terms "confidence token(s)" or "calibration token(s)" as a named mechanism comparable to Self-RAG's reflection tokens.
- Could not verify whether any paper has directly measured whether token-based abstention (vs. NL-phrase abstention) is more robust/less "jailbreakable" or more consistently interpretable by downstream systems — this looks like an open question.

## Is there published work on a "DONE"/"finished retrieving" control token for agentic retrieval stopping criteria?

### Takeaway
Explicit end-of-retrieval control tokens do exist in RL-trained agentic-search systems (Search-R1 and descendants use `<search>...</search>` tags and reasoning/answer tags to structure when retrieval happens and when the model moves to answering), but the "stopping" decision in most current agentic RAG stopping-criteria research (2025–2026) is handled by *implicit* policy learning, confidence thresholds, or answer-convergence heuristics rather than by training a single dedicated "DONE" token whose emission is directly supervised as a stopping signal — and at least one recent paper explicitly argues that treating "finishing" as just another emitted token and hoping large-scale RL training implicitly teaches correct stopping is unreliable.

### Cited Findings
- Search-R1 (RL-trained retrieval-augmented reasoning model) structures generation with special/structural tokens: `<think>...</think>` for internal reasoning, `<search>query</search>` to trigger retrieval, and answer tags; retrieved-token positions are gradient-masked during RL so only the model's own decision tokens (including when to search vs. when to stop and answer) are optimized — [emergentmind Search-R1 summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/search-r1), [venturebeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/beyond-rag-search-r1-integrates-search-engines-directly-into-reasoning-models)
- A 2026 paper, "When Should Multi-Round RAG Stop? Structured Stopping Judgments and Retrieval Reduction in Search-R1" (arXiv:2608.13237), directly studies the stopping-criterion problem in Search-R1-style systems, and states that existing approaches "often treat finishing as just another tool call, expecting large-scale training to implicitly teach models when to stop," and argues this is unreliable because "the model feels done" is not by itself a trustworthy signal — the paper proposes more structured stopping judgments instead — [arXiv:2608.13237](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13237)
- "Stop-RAG: Value-Based Retrieval Control for Iterative RAG" (arXiv:2510.14337) frames the stop/continue decision as a value-estimation problem (RL value function) rather than a discrete emitted token — [arXiv:2510.14337](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14337)
- "TASR: Training-Free Adaptive Stopping for Iterative Retrieval" (arXiv:2606.13814) stops iterative retrieval loops based on answer convergence (the model repeating its previous round's normalized answer) combined with a calibrated probability-correct threshold — explicitly training-free, i.e. not a learned control token — [arXiv:2606.13814](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13814)
- Other described stopping mechanisms in the agentic-RAG literature include fixed step/tool-call budgets (e.g., "at most K SEARCH calls"), token budgets, and confidence-based stopping using logit margins/entropy/self-consistency disagreement — [arXiv:2601.00536 survey summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.00536)

### Inferences
- A dedicated, explicitly-trained "DONE" token analogous to Self-RAG's `[Retrieve]=no` but as its own first-class control symbol for ending an agentic retrieval loop is architecturally plausible and partially present (Search-R1's structural tags implicitly encode a stop-and-answer transition), but the current published emphasis (as of the papers found, 2025–2026) is shifting *away* from relying on an implicitly-trained "done" signal and toward explicit auxiliary stopping judgments, value functions, or training-free calibrated heuristics — because researchers found bare reliance on the model's own learned stop token/feeling to be insufficiently reliable.

### Gaps
- No paper found that names or evaluates a single dedicated "DONE" token in isolation as the stopping mechanism (i.e., ablating "DONE-token-only" stopping against value-function or budget-based stopping). The arXiv:2608.13237 finding that implicit token-based stopping is unreliable is the most directly relevant caution but is about Search-R1-style tag-based stopping broadly, not a literal single "DONE" token scheme.

## PART B: Does quantization increase hallucination / decrease faithfulness?

### Takeaway
There is now a real and growing (2024–2026) body of empirical work — not just anecdote — showing that low-bit post-training quantization (especially 4-bit and below) measurably increases hallucination and reduces factual/truthfulness reliability compared to FP16, even when standard benchmarks (perplexity, general accuracy) show little to no degradation. The effect is consistently reported as non-linear: 8-bit is described repeatedly as "minimal impact," while 4-bit is repeatedly flagged as the threshold where hallucination/factuality/truthfulness degradation becomes clearly measurable and sometimes severe, with disproportionate impact on smaller models, non-English languages, and knowledge-intensive/reasoning-heavy tasks. However, this search did not find a study that isolates RAG-specific *citation accuracy/groundedness* across quantization levels as its primary object — most of the direct quantization-hallucination evidence is about parametric/factual-recall hallucination (open-domain QA, package/API hallucination, biomedical/financial domain QA), not citation-grounded RAG pipelines specifically.

### Cited Findings
- Multiple sources (aggregated in search) state: "Quantization at low bit-widths significantly increases hallucinations; experiments on Llama2-Chat show minimal impact at 8-bit but noticeable increases in hallucination rates at 4-bit across domains including biomedicine and finance" and describe testing Llama-2-Chat 7B and 13B at 4-bit, 8-bit, and 16-bit precision — [search synthesis citing arXiv:2401.03205 "The Dawn After the Dark"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.03205) (NOTE: direct PDF fetch of this paper failed/returned corrupted text in this session — see Gaps)
- "Quantized but Deceptive? A Multi-Dimensional Truthfulness Evaluation of Quantized LLMs" (arXiv:2508.19432, 2025) evaluates GPTQ, AWQ, and AQLM quantization methods on Llama 2, Llama 3, Mistral, and Qwen 2, using a multi-dimensional truthfulness/factuality evaluation framework; its central finding is that "quantized models can achieve similar performance on conventional metrics while exhibiting deceptive behaviors" regarding factual accuracy — i.e., precision of factual responses degrades at higher compression even when perplexity-style metrics suggest minimal degradation, meaning standard benchmarks under-detect the reliability cost of quantization — [arXiv:2508.19432](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19432)
- "Secure or Suspect? Investigating Package Hallucinations of Shell Command in Original and Quantized LLMs" (arXiv:2512.08213, 2025) finds quantization systematically increases package/command hallucination, characterizing 8-bit as "a measurable but often acceptable decline in accuracy" while "4-bit quantization poses a fundamental threat to functional reliability" — [arXiv:2512.08213](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08213)
- "Compressing LLMs: The Truth is Rarely Pure and Never Simple" (Jaiswal et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.01382) is a methodological/critical paper arguing that perplexity — the metric most compression papers rely on — masks real degradation; the authors introduce "LLM-KICK," a more holistic evaluation suite covering language understanding, reasoning, generation, in-context retrieval, and in-context summarization, and find that "all pruning methods suffer significant performance degradation, sometimes at trivial sparsity ratios (25–30%)," while noting quantization methods are comparatively more successful than pruning at preserving these capabilities — this paper is about compression broadly (pruning + quantization) and does not focus specifically on "hallucination" as a named metric, but is the key methodological precedent for "perplexity hides real degradation," which later hallucination-specific papers (e.g. arXiv:2508.19432) build on — [arXiv:2310.01382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01382), [Apple ML Research summary](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/compressing-llms)
- "How Does Quantization Affect Multilingual LLMs?" (arXiv:2407.03211, EMNLP Findings 2024) finds that automatic metrics substantially *underestimate* real damage from quantization: e.g., a 1.7% average automatic-metric drop in Japanese corresponded to a 16.0% drop as judged by human evaluators on realistic prompts; the damage is uneven across languages (non-Latin-script languages more severely affected), and task-specific degradation is severe for challenging tasks like mathematical reasoning (a 35B model with W4-g quantization saw -13.1% relative performance on MGSM, with Chinese as low as -17.3%) — [arXiv:2407.03211](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03211), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.935/)
- "Quantization Undoes Alignment: Bias Emergence in Compressed LLMs Across Models and Precision Levels" (arXiv:2605.15208, 2026) is a related but distinct line — showing that quantization can degrade *alignment* (bias/safety behavior) across precision levels, which is adjacent to but not the same claim as hallucination/factuality degradation; flagged here as related evidence of quantization's broader "silent" behavioral costs — [arXiv:2605.15208](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15208)
- "Hallucination Is Linearly Decodable from Mid-Layer Hidden States in Quantized LLMs" (arXiv:2606.02628, 2026) and "Empirical Analysis of Internal Hallucination Detection in Quantized LLMs: Layer Dynamics and White-Box Benchmarks" (MDPI, arXiv-adjacent) both treat quantized models as a specific object of study for hallucination-detection research, implying the research community treats quantized-model hallucination as an established enough phenomenon to build detection tooling around — [arXiv:2606.02628](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02628), [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/15/9/1802)
- "Quantized Large Language Models in Biomedical Natural Language Processing: Evaluation and Recommendation" (arXiv:2509.04534, 2025) is a domain-specific (biomedical) evaluation of quantized LLMs, consistent with the pattern that knowledge-intensive specialist domains are a common testbed for showing quantization-driven factuality loss — [arXiv:2509.04534](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.04534)
- On RAG-specific quantization work: search surfaced that some RAG evaluation studies use 4-bit-quantized large models (e.g., Cohere Command-R+ 104B) simply as a practical necessity to fit on available hardware (single A100) for running RAG evaluations — this is evidence quantization is common practice in RAG deployment/research, not evidence of a dedicated study comparing citation-accuracy/groundedness *across* quantization levels of the *same* base model — [search synthesis, RAG evaluation blog aggregation](https://www.openlayer.com/blog/rag-pipeline-evaluation-groundedness-faithfulness)
- General RAG evaluation literature establishes the relevant metrics that a quantization-vs-RAG-faithfulness study would need (faithfulness, groundedness, citation precision/recall), citing frameworks RAGAS, ARES, FActScore, TRUVA, and QAFactEval, and notes an important conceptual distinction: "a response can be grounded but unfaithful by misrepresenting what context says, or faithful to what it cites but ungrounded by accurately quoting a source that was never retrieved" (from "Correctness is not Faithfulness in Retrieval Augmented Generation Attributions," arXiv:2412.18004) — this is useful evaluation-methodology context but is not itself a quantization study — [arXiv:2412.18004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.18004)

### Inferences
- The claim "quantization increases hallucination, especially below 8-bit" is well-established across at least 4–5 independent 2024–2026 papers using different models (Llama-2, Llama-3, Mistral, Qwen-2) and different quantization methods (GPTQ, AWQ, AQLM, plus generic W4/W8 bitsandbytes-style setups), so this should be treated as a credible, converging empirical finding rather than a single-paper claim or blog-level anecdote.
- The specific narrower claim the user is likely most interested in — "does quantization reduce RAG citation accuracy/groundedness specifically" — is NOT directly established by any single source found in this search. The closest adjacent evidence is: (a) general factuality/truthfulness degrades under quantization (multiple papers), and (b) RAG faithfulness/groundedness is a distinct, well-instrumented metric family (RAGAS/ARES/FActScore/TRUVA) that *could* be applied to a quantization study but does not appear to have been in a dedicated published paper found here. This should be flagged to the report writer as a genuine, currently-unaddressed research gap, not just an artifact of limited search — it is consistent with there being room for a novel study on "does quantization degrade RAG-specific groundedness/citation accuracy" as opposed to general parametric-knowledge hallucination.
- The "perplexity/standard-benchmark metrics under-detect real degradation" finding (Jaiswal et al. ICLR 2024; arXiv:2508.19432; arXiv:2407.03211's human-vs-automatic gap) is a load-bearing point for anyone using quantized models in production RAG systems: absence of measured perplexity/accuracy regression after quantization is not sufficient evidence that faithfulness/hallucination behavior is unaffected.

### Gaps
- Could not fully verify the exact quantitative findings of "The Dawn After the Dark: An Empirical Study on Factuality Hallucination in Large Language Models" (arXiv:2401.03205) — the direct PDF fetch failed/returned corrupted binary data in this session, so the specific 4-bit/8-bit/16-bit numeric hallucination-rate deltas by domain (biomedicine vs. finance) are reported here only via aggregated search-engine synthesis, not a verified primary-source read. This should be re-verified from the primary source (https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03205 or the ACL Anthology version, arXiv:2401.03205 = ACL 2024 companion of 2024.acl-long.586) before being cited as a hard number in a final report.
- No dedicated peer-reviewed or credible technical-blog study found that benchmarks RAG-pipeline citation accuracy or groundedness specifically across quantization levels (FP16 vs INT8 vs INT4/GGUF Q4) of the *same* base model. This appears to be a genuine open gap in the literature as of this search (September 2026), not merely a search-coverage failure — related communities (RAG evaluation people, and quantization-hallucination people) appear to not yet have intersected on this specific question.
- Did not find independent-lab technical benchmarks from Neural Magic, Answer.AI, EleutherAI, or similar orgs specifically on hallucination/faithfulness (as opposed to speed/perplexity/general-accuracy benchmarks) — this search did not surface such blog posts; it's unclear whether they don't exist or simply didn't surface under these queries, so this should be treated as "not found" rather than "confirmed absent."
- GGUF/llama.cpp-specific and bitsandbytes-specific (as opposed to GPTQ/AWQ/AQLM) hallucination studies were not distinctly surfaced; most academic papers found test GPTQ/AWQ/AQLM or generic bit-width settings rather than naming llama.cpp/GGUF or bitsandbytes explicitly, so it's unclear whether findings transfer identically to those specific quantization toolchains.
